When we talk about cinema that truly makes you *think*, the 2014 Bollywood masala vehicle *It's Entertainment* isn't usually the first title that springs to mind. Directed by Sajid and Farhad Samji, this action-comedy-drama, starring Akshay Kumar and, notably, a Golden Retriever named Junior, was largely met with a shrug by critics. Reviews often panned its convoluted, often outright nonsensical plot, its reliance on slapstick, and a runtime of 140 minutes that tested the patience of many. Yet, beneath the layers of broad comedy and improbable twists, there's an unwitting, almost accidental philosophical core — a chaotic mirror reflecting humanity's most primal desires and absurd aspirations.

## The Absurdity of Fortune and Human Folly

The premise of *It's Entertainment* is, without mincing words, utterly ridiculous: Akhil (Akshay Kumar), a down-on-his-luck young man, discovers his biological father was a wealthy diamond merchant. But before he can claim his rightful inheritance, he learns the patriarch has bequeathed his entire fortune to his beloved dog, Entertainment. Yes, a dog. This setup alone, which many critics rightfully highlighted as the film's biggest weakness, becomes its most intriguing philosophical point.

The film instantly plunges us into a world defined by **arbitrary wealth** and **existential envy**. Akhil's entire life is a struggle, and suddenly, the universe dangles untold riches before him, only to snatch them away via a canine beneficiary. This isn't just a plot device; it's a profound statement on the inherent randomness of fate and the arbitrary nature of value. Why does one person inherit millions while another toils? And why, in this specific instance, is a creature entirely oblivious to monetary value suddenly the lynchpin of human desire and despair?

The common criticism of the plot's illogical nature, while valid from a narrative craft perspective, inadvertently highlights the film's philosophical power. The very absurdity of a dog inheriting a fortune forces us to confront our own human-centric views on wealth, ownership, and legacy. What makes *our* claims to inheritance more valid than a dog's, beyond our self-assigned intellectual superiority? The film, however clumsily, nudges us toward a kind of **cosmic humor** where human avarice is exposed as a grand, often pathetic, charade. Akhil and his associates' desperate, often unethical, schemes to wrest the fortune from Entertainment become a comedic yet stark portrayal of **human greed** and the lengths to which we'll go for material gain.

## The Masala Paradox: Entertainment vs. Substance

*It's Entertainment* is, at its heart, a Bollywood masala film, a genre known for its blend of action, comedy, romance, and drama, often with a generous dash of song and dance. For many, its primary goal was simply to entertain, to provide a few hours of lighthearted escapism. And for some audiences, it succeeded in this, with Akshay Kumar's comedic timing often singled out as a highlight, alongside Junior's charming performance.

> The true purpose of "entertainment" isn't always to provoke deep thought, but sometimes, in its very lack of seriousness, it can inadvertently reveal the profound absurdities of our existence.
{: .prompt-info }

However, critics often found this "masala" mix to be uneven, the comedy forced, and the emotional beats underdeveloped. The pacing issues common in such films, where songs often interrupt narrative flow, were also noted. This brings us to a crucial philosophical question about the **purpose of entertainment**. Does a film need to be critically acclaimed or narratively tight to offer philosophical insight? Or can its very flaws, its blatant pursuit of "entertainment" for entertainment's sake, paradoxically expose deeper truths?

The film's title, *It's Entertainment*, is almost a self-referential wink, a declaration of its own intent. It invites us to turn off our critical faculties and simply enjoy the spectacle. Yet, in doing so, it allows us to observe the characters' frantic pursuit of wealth without the usual dramatic gravitas. The film's light tone prevents us from taking the characters' moral compromises *too* seriously, yet we still witness them. This creates a fascinating distance, allowing the viewer to laugh at the folly while simultaneously recognizing the underlying, very human, motivations of insecurity, ambition, and the desire for an easier life. The performances, particularly that of the dog, Junior, often garnered genuine affection, proving that even in the most outlandish plots, a connection to the human (or canine) heart can still be forged, transcending the script's limitations.

## What Does a Dog Know of Inheritance?

The central conceit of a dog inheriting a fortune isn't just a comedic setup; it's a potent philosophical symbol. Entertainment, the dog, is a creature of pure instinct and simple needs. He knows nothing of money, power, or social standing. He is, in a sense, a blank slate onto which human desires are projected. His inheritance highlights the stark contrast between our complex, often distorted, value systems and the uncomplicated reality of an animal.

This raises questions about **anthropocentrism** – our human-centric view of the world. We assume that wealth, legacy, and inheritance are inherently human constructs, meant for human beneficiaries. But by placing a dog at the center of this financial drama, the film, perhaps unintentionally, challenges this assumption. It asks: What is wealth, truly, outside of human societal agreement? To Entertainment, the fortune is meaningless. It provides no more comfort than a kind owner and a bowl of kibble. This underscores the **illusory nature of material value** and how much of our suffering and joy is tied to abstract concepts we ourselves have created.

The dog becomes a silent, innocent judge of human character. He reflects the avarice, the cunning, and the desperation of Akhil, Krishna Abhishek's character, and others who scheme around him. He is a mirror, clean and unblemished by human greed, reflecting back the ugliness of those who covet his meaningless (to him) fortune. In a strange, indirect way, *It's Entertainment* forces us to consider our own relationship with wealth and what truly constitutes a "good life." Is it the accumulation of riches, or is it the simple, unburdened existence of a loyal companion, whose only concerns are love and sustenance?

> While *It's Entertainment* might never grace the syllabi of film studies programs, its chaotic embrace of the absurd and its unapologetic pursuit of unadulterated escapism inadvertently peels back layers of our collective anxieties about wealth, legacy, and the true meaning of a life well-lived.
{: .prompt-warning }

*It's Entertainment* may have been a critical punching bag and a divisive film for audiences, often criticized for its loud comedy and convoluted plot. But in its very commitment to the outlandish, it stumbles upon a surprising amount of philosophical fodder. It's a testament to the idea that even the most "flawed" pieces of art can, through their very imperfections, spark profound questions about our humanity. It might not be a masterpiece, but it's certainly *something*. And that "something" is a chaotic, comedic, yet curiously poignant reflection on the human condition, always chasing fortunes, even those held by a golden retriever.
